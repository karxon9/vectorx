Triangle Law Explained

"Triangle Law of Vector Addition": When two vectors are arranged head-to-tail so they represent two adjacent sides of a triangle in continuous order, their vector sum (the Resultant $\vec{R}$) is represented both in magnitude and direction by the closing side of the triangle drawn from the initial tail of $\vec{A}$ to the final head of $\vec{B}$.

Head-to-Tail Rule:

Tail of $\vec{B}$ must touch Head of $\vec{A}$. The resultant $\vec{R}$ starts at Tail($\vec{A}$) and ends at Head($\vec{B}$).

How to use the simulator

  1. Vector Movement: Click and drag anywhere along the stem of Vector A or Vector B to translate it across the canvas.
  2. Rotate / Resize: Drag the arrow tips (heads) or bases (tails) to rotate and adjust vector length.
  3. Make the Connection: Drag Vector B's tail close to Vector A's arrow tip. Within the snap range (~30px), Vector B will magnetically snap into place!
  4. Observe Resultant: Instantly, the closing green arrow R = A + B is drawn with particle effects, computing exact component values in real time.
